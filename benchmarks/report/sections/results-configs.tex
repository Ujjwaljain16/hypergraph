\subsection{Larger data: scale 4}
\label{sec:scale4}

At scale 4 the dataset has \M{incidencesC} incidences, and the files of both systems are several times larger than their 64\,MiB caches. Most of HStore's leads hold or grow. Hyperedge insertion becomes \M{sumCingestedges} faster (\M{hCingestedges} against \M{gCingestedges} per second), batched updates \M{sumCwriteupdate} and single-update commits \M{sumClatencycommit}, the last with overlapping ranges. Co-membership counts are \M{sumCreadcomembership} faster, incidence lookups \M{sumCreadincidence} (ranges overlapping), and cold lookups after a reopen \M{sumCreadincidencecold}.

Two read workloads reverse. HStore enumerates the members of \M{hCreadmembers} hyperedges per second against HyperGraphDB's \M{gCreadmembers}, a ratio of \M{sumCreadmembers}. Two-hop neighbourhoods, which enumerate the members of every incident hyperedge, follow at \M{sumCreadtwohop}. The resource counters show why. At scale 4 HStore's member lookups miss its node cache: each one reads about 778 bytes from the files and allocates \M{allocChstorereadmembers} while decoding the catalog page that holds the edge record, and a two-hop query allocates \M{allocChstorereadtwohop}. HyperGraphDB serves the same lookups from its atom cache, which holds each link's targets as a Java array and is not limited by the 64\,MiB budget: it allocates \M{allocChgdbreadmembers} per member lookup. \Cref{sec:discussion} returns to this.

On disk, HyperGraphDB's files after loading are \M{gCdisk} against HStore's \M{hCdisk}. After the deletes, HStore's files grow to \M{hCdiskchurned}. The deletes rewrite a large part of the incidence index, and the replaced pages remain reachable from the 64 retained generations until the next restart releases that history. The benchmark restarts the store after the deletes, and during the 10\,s \wl{compaction.baseline} window that follows, HStore's background maintenance released and compacted that space. When the measured compaction window began, the explicit compaction took \M{compmsChstore} and found almost nothing left to reclaim, and the final size was \M{hCdiskcompacted}. JE's cleaner ran for \M{compmsChgdb} in the same window and brought HyperGraphDB from \M{gCdiskchurned} to \M{gCdiskcompacted}.

A clean reopen is \M{sumCreopen} faster in HStore, and opening after a crash takes \M{hCrecover} against \M{gCrecover}. Over the whole run, HStore wrote \M{totalwrittenChstore} and HyperGraphDB \M{totalwrittenChgdb}, most of it during cleaning. HStore's single-update commits have a lower p99 (\M{latChstorelatencycommitptwo} against \M{latChgdblatencycommitptwo}) and p99.9, but a higher maximum: \M{latChstorelatencycommitmax} against \M{latChgdblatencycommitmax}.
