\subsection{Synchronous commits}
\label{sec:sync}

With synchronous commits, every acknowledged commit has been forced to stable storage. On this machine a forced write costs about 4\,ms, and it dominates single-update transactions in both systems. HStore commits \M{hBlatencycommit} of them per second and HyperGraphDB \M{gBlatencycommit} (\M{sumBlatencycommit}). The medians are \M{latBhstorelatencycommitmed} and \M{latBhgdblatencycommitmed}, one forced write each, and HStore's lead comes from the tail: \M{latBhstorelatencycommitpthree} against \M{latBhgdblatencycommitpthree} at p99.9.

Batched work spreads the forced write over 1,000 operations. HStore inserts nodes \M{sumBingestnodes} as fast as HyperGraphDB and hyperedges \M{sumBingestedges} as fast (ranges overlapping), and updates values \M{sumBwriteupdate} as fast. The open-loop writer of \wl{mixed} commits 10 updates every 10\,ms. HStore held that schedule, with a median commit latency of \M{latBhstoremixedwritemed}. HyperGraphDB fell behind it: its commits waited \M{latBhgdbmixedwritemed} at the median, and its slowest run reached \M{mixminBhgdb} updates per second.

Under synchronous commits, JE reports about 210\,KiB written per single-update commit, against about 9\,KiB with asynchronous commits. The extra bytes come with the log flush that the adapter adds (\cref{sec:durability}). We have not established what JE writes in that flush, so the bytes-written figures for synchronous single commits in \cref{app:full} should not be read as a comparison.

Reads do not commit, and HStore leads every read workload in this configuration as well (\cref{tab:summary}). Recovery after SIGKILL takes \M{hBrecover} in HStore and \M{gBrecover} in HyperGraphDB at the median. The ranges overlap, and one HStore run took 1.6\,s. As in every other configuration, no run of either system lost an acknowledged commit.
